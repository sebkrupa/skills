# Interview question bank

Use these as a pool, not a script. Each next question must follow from the last answer (full value listening). Use the client's exact words when you summarize; do not swap in synonyms. End each summary with "Did I miss anything? Is this correct?"

## Probes (use when an answer is vague)
- "What else?" … "And what is the real challenge for you?" (Michael Bungay Stanier)
- "On a scale of 1-10, how important is this? Why is it not lower?" (the user argues for it)
- "Why not leave it as it is? What does it cost you if nothing changes in 12 months?"
- "Give me the exact words a client used." / "Name one real client like that."
- "If you could only keep one of these, which one?"
- "What would a competitor say about this answer?"
- Stuck on abstract words ("welcoming", "premium", "modern")? Stop talking. Show 2-3 contrasting examples (brands, sites, stylescapes) and ask which is closest and why. Reaction to real samples is faster and truer than definitions.
- Ask broad first, narrow later (onion peeling; 21 questions: each question should cut the options in half). Do not ask about scope, pages, fonts or platforms before the problem and the motive are clear.

## A. Where we are
1. Brand type: personal brand, service business or agency, or product or e-commerce brand? Name, URL, profiles.
2. In one sentence each: What do you do? For whom? Why does it matter?
3. Why are you working on the brand now? What triggered it? What happens if you do nothing?
4. Goal in 12 months, in numbers: revenue, number of clients, price per client, hours per week, reach. And the personal goal behind it.
5. Current numbers: revenue, number of clients, average order or project value, lifetime value, where clients come from (referral, search, social, ads), cost per client if known, close rate.
6. Hours per week and income per week. Which activities bring the money (the top 4%)? What could you delegate?
7. Main bottleneck: too few people know you (small list, low traffic), or they know you but do not buy (weak offer, low conversion)?
8. What do clients praise you for, in their words? What do they complain about? What do people say about you when you are not in the room?
9. What did you try in marketing already? What worked, what failed, and why do you think so?
10. Who do you see as competitors? Who do clients compare you with? What else could the client do instead (DIY, an employee, nothing)?
11. Constraints: budget for marketing, time per week, skills (writing, video, camera comfort), team, legal limits, things you will never do.
12. Existing brand assets: name, logo, colors, slogans, website, content, testimonials, case studies. What do you want to keep?

## B. Who we serve
See `ideal-client.md` for the full method. Core questions:
1. Your best 20% of clients (profit, joy, referrals). Who are the top 4%? What do they have in common beyond age and location?
2. Which clients do you never want again? Why?
3. Dream buyer questions (where they hang out, where they search, frustrations, hopes, fears, contact preference, exact phrases, day in the life, what makes them happy).
4. Before and after grid: have, feel, average day, status, good versus evil.
5. Their three options: hire you, hire someone else, do nothing. Why would they pick each?
6. Who decides and who pays? Who else influences the decision?
7. Functional, emotional and social job: what are they trying to get done, how do they want to feel, how do they want to be seen?

## C. Where we could be
1. What can you be the best in the world at, or the only one doing? What would you do even if no one paid?
2. If you had to keep one service, one industry or one problem, which one? What would you drop first?
3. What do you believe about your industry that most competitors do not? What do you fight against (the enemy)?
4. Origin story: the struggle, the turning point, the decision, the mentor.
5. Expertise level today: unknown, expert, authority (other experts name you), influencer (known outside the industry)? Proof?
6. Where do you want to be in 3 years? Where do you refuse to go?
7. Personal brand only: shadow questions in `personal-brand.md`.

## D. What we sell
1. Full list of features of the main offer. Turn each into a benefit for the client.
2. What result can you promise, in what time, and against what objection (ROT)?
3. What guarantee could you give that makes you a little nervous?
4. Price today and how it was set. What is the result worth to the client in money?
5. Entry offer, core offer, premium offer, ongoing offer? What does the client buy next?
6. Real limits (capacity, dates, stock) that can back urgency honestly.
7. Top 5 objections (experience, industry knowledge, price, timing, team size) and the answer to each.

## E. How we are heard
1. Three adjectives for how the brand should feel. Three it must never feel like.
2. Brands or people whose voice you like. Why?
3. Where are you comfortable: writing, speaking, video, live events, one to one?
4. Channels you use now, with results. Channels the ideal client actually uses.
5. Who already serves your ideal client without competing with you (referral partners, big fish)?
6. Website: goal of the site (book a call, buy, apply, subscribe)? What must a visitor understand in 5 seconds?
7. What proof can you publish: numbers, case studies, logos, testimonials, press, awards?

## F. Where we should not be
1. Which clients, projects, markets or channels cost you more than they give?
2. Which services do you offer only "because clients ask"?
3. Which messages or claims would every competitor also make? (Drop them.)
4. What is the brand risk you fear most (looking cheap, looking arrogant, looking like everyone else)?
