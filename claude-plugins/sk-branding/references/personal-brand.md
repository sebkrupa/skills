# Personal brand (Chris Do, Adobe MAX 2023, and The Futur)

## Paradox
Companies try to look human; people act like bland companies. People fall in love with distinct personalities, not sterile institutions. Trying to please everyone and avoid any edge kills a personal brand. Example: Aaron Draplin opens a TEDx talk with his age, his lack of a degree, his weight and the fact that he does not own long trousers.

## Karmic equity
- Building a brand means giving value without asking for anything. Each generous act is a deposit; it compounds while you ask for nothing.
- Each direct ask (buy, sign up, hire me) withdraws from the account. Ask rarely, and only after many deposits.
- Old rule: make a product for everyone and tell your own story. New rule: learn the client's story, make what they want, and give them a story to retell.
- Performance marketing brings fast money but can erode long-term brand value. Example: Reese Witherspoon's book club (2.5 million readers) helped sell Hello Sunshine for $900M.

## Shadow work (Carl Jung's persona and shadow)
Authentic leaders combine confidence, self-awareness and willingness to show weakness. Vulnerability is the price of deep connection.
Template:
1. How most people see me: <outer persona>
2. What they do not know: <hidden weakness I really struggle with>
3. Lesson from the shadow: <how it made me stronger>
5% rule: hide 90% of the daily routine; show the top 5% wins and the worst 5% failures. Example: Chris Do posting about shaking knees while accepting an Emmy.
Watch for cultural limits (Law of Jante in Scandinavia: do not stand out). Name the limit, then decide consciously.

## Two-word brand
Complexity kills understanding, memory and action.
Formula: `[Transformer word] + [Shadow word]`.
- Shadow word: a hidden flaw, inner conflict or an insult critics use.
- Transformer word: changes the meaning, turning the flaw into a market asset.
- The result should make people ask "what does that mean?"
- Avoid cute names that sound clever but carry no emotional truth.
Examples: "attention seeker" → Attention Broker; old age → Brandmother; "greedy" → Goal Digger; Proud People Pleaser (Design Cuts founder); Noble Assassin; Growth Writer (from ghostwriter); Yeasty Boys (bakery truck); Primal Swoldier.

## Identity elements to collect
- Origin: birthplace, parents, past (cultural capital; city stereotypes help).
- Community: allies, sidekicks, city.
- Defining traits: quirks, catchphrases.
- The enemy: what you fight and want to end.

## Origin story
- The hero is born at a hard moral decision in the face of loss, not at the moment of getting the power. Peter Parker got powers from the bite but became Spider-Man after uncle Ben's death.
- Hero vs villain: the hero overcomes the hurt and grows; the villain stays a victim and seeks revenge.
- A mentor shapes values, often through early resistance. Template: who shaped my thinking; what I learned; how I resisted at first.

## Storytelling: 3F
Fact (data), Feeling (shared emotion), Fun (humor or wordplay). Example: The Ugly Company's dried peaches: food-waste statistics, "don't judge a fruit by its cover", and "pit happens".

## Visual signature
- Style is making the same "mistake" long enough.
- A recognizable silhouette, one repeated color accent, and accessories built from a physical trait. Example: Chris Do turns baldness and poor sight into signature hats and thick glasses.
- A handwritten signature or monogram can become the personal logo.
- Your name and identity are the root for naming projects, products and community.

## Personal profile over company profile
On social media a person beats a logo. Company pages tend to post boring ads. Build the founder's profile and let the company ride on it (Brendan Kane; Tanner Leatherstein grew from 2,000 to 2.5 million followers with education, no direct sales asks).
