# Voice, tone and copy

## 1. Voice and tone
- Voice is constant; tone changes with the situation (sale, complaint, celebration).
- Tone sliders (external: Nielsen Norman Group): funny ↔ serious, formal ↔ casual, respectful ↔ irreverent, enthusiastic ↔ matter-of-fact. Place the brand on each with a number 1-5.
- Archetype as a shortcut (external: 12 Jungian brand archetypes, e.g. Sage, Hero, Outlaw, Caregiver, Creator, Explorer): pick one main and one secondary. Use it to keep tone consistent, not as decoration.
- Write: 3 adjectives the brand is, 3 it is never; words to use, words to avoid; 3 rewrites of real texts (before → after).
- "Dear Son" frame (Jasmin Alic): draft starting "Dear Son," and ending "Love, Dad", then delete both. It forces simple words, short sentences, a human tone and one reader.
- Speak to one person in bed with a phone, not to a crowd. No "Hi everyone" (Brendan Kane).
- Talk like a friend. Share some of your life ("let them see your blemishes"), then give value. Be polarizing rather than bland. Never lie, never trick, never insult the reader's intelligence (Suby).
- Customer's point of view: say what the product does for them, not what it is ("wooden coasters" → "protect your table with coasters engraved with your family name").

## 2. Headlines and hooks
- ROT (Chris Do): Result + Objection + Time. "How to get <result> without <objection> in <time>." Any order. The result can be emotional ("a logo your wife will love"). In hard-to-measure fields use a success rate ("80% of my patients are back in form in 2 weeks"). Sell the end state, not the process ("work 3 days a week", not "delegation").
- Opt-in formulas (Suby): "Finally! How to get <result> without <fear> in <time> – guaranteed." / "<Problem>? Get my <solution> and <result>."
- Title formulas (Suby): "X ways to <desire> without <undesired thing>"; "<Hard thing> in <time> even if <shortcoming>"; "<Desire> like <expert> even without <expected thing>"; "How to eliminate <biggest problem> …".
- Headline essentials: grab attention (five times as many read the headline as the body, Ogilvy); numbers; intrigue (power words: must, shocking, exposed, revealed, secret, confession); show what is in it for them; be specific about cost or consequence. Boring: "5 facts about web design". Strong: "22 money-murdering mistakes no web designer will tell you".
- Fascination bullets: "How to X without Y"; "You need X, right? Wrong!"; "Where to find Y"; "What you should never …" (fear of loss beats hope of gain); "Say goodbye to X"; "The truth about …".
- Primal hooks for ads: curiosity, shock, direct benefit, implied benefit, fear, vanity, self-interest (better, richer, faster, healthier, happier, smarter).
- Hook types from the before and after grid: agitate the pain, or show the transformation.
- Hook + rehook (LinkedIn): line 1 is a clear promise (no bait); line 2 kills an objection ("Without $0 investment") or sets a contrast ("…but it is the worst platform to sell on"). Question for the hook: "What is the one thing the reader should learn?"
- Write 50 opening lines, score each 1-5, keep the best 5-10 (Brendan Kane).

## 3. Slogan styles to offer
1. Result: ROT-based promise.
2. Enemy or contrast: what you stand against.
3. Identity: who the client becomes (status), or a two-word brand.
4. Simple formula for product copy: pain + benefit + call to action.
Test each: specific? true? could a competitor sign it? does it make the reader ask "how?"

## 4. Page and email structure
- AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action.
- Sales page sequence (Suby): call out the audience → big-promise headline → subheadline backing it → 6 best bullets (each a different pain, fear, hope or dream) → floodlight and agitate the problem, why past attempts failed → the solution and how it differs → credentials and proof → benefits → social proof → offer → guarantee → urgency → CTA.
- StoryBrand SB7 (external: Donald Miller): the client is the hero, not you. Character with a problem (external, internal, philosophical) meets a guide (you: empathy + authority) who gives a plan (3 steps) and calls to action; show the failure avoided and the success gained.
- Three ways to influence: say you are good; let others say it; show it. Showing works best: help them first.
- Long copy works when it is interesting. Buyers read.
- CTA is a command ("Book your call"), not a question.
- Email: subject 1-20 characters or very long (avoid the middle), lower case, personal-looking; preheader with a cliffhanger; lead (pattern interrupt) → story → CTA. Plain text, a personal sender, logo in the signature. Make it visceral ("increase sales" → "imagine doubling sales in 90 days and not worrying on weekends").

## 5. Storytelling
- Founder story: struggle, turning point, mission. People love it; it builds trust.
- 3F: fact, feeling, fun.
- Product story: "This board was made for a 25th wedding anniversary."
- Brand as movement: belief, ritual, tribe language (Apple keynotes, stores as temples). Honest version only.

## 6. Accessibility in social posts
No fake bold or italic Unicode generators (screen readers read gibberish). Emoji only at line ends. No whole words in capitals except common acronyms.
