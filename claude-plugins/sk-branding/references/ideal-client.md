# Ideal client

Rule: an avatar made only of age, gender and location matches what every competitor has. Go deeper: daily life, status, exact words, fears.
Personas also take personal taste out of design and copy decisions: "Would <persona> respond to this?" is the filter for every later choice.

## 1. Find the best clients (Halo Strategy, Sabri Suby)
1. Find the 20% of clients who bring 80% of sales and profit. Inside them, the "power 4%" (about 64% of revenue).
2. List what they share beyond demographics: which offer they asked about, which channel they came from, what triggered the purchase.
3. Detective work: take 2-3 main keywords. Read Google results and autocomplete, AnswerThePublic, Reddit, Quora, YouTube comments, Amazon and Google reviews of competitors, LinkedIn and Facebook groups. Note what people like, hate, ask, and the problems where they keep "banging their head against the wall". Copy their exact words.
4. Find the gaps: what competitors leave unanswered. "This gap is your winning ticket."
5. Write a one-paragraph portrait of the dream buyer.

Also define the anti-client. Ideal clients bring energy, better work and similar referrals. Wrong-fit clients bring resentment and a negative spiral (Joanna Galvao). Set hard entry criteria.

## 2. Dream buyer questions (Sabri Suby)
Be specific. Not "uses Facebook" but "takes her two kids to the park every Saturday morning".
1. Where do they hang out, online and offline?
2. Where do they look for information? (One sentence: "When Sally is curious, she searches Google on her iPhone.")
3. Biggest frustrations and challenges? (Most important. Write in first person: "I need to …")
4. Hopes, dreams and desires? (Sell the promised land.)
5. Biggest fears?
6. Preferred contact: email, text, chat, live video, post, phone?
7. Exact phrases and words they use? Target reaction: "It's like they're talking directly to me."
8. A day in their life, hour by hour? (A Monday 8am person differs from a Friday 6:30pm person.)
9. What makes them happy?

## 3. Personas (The Futur, brewery example)
Make 2-3 personas, each with a name, age, family, job, income, where they shop, what they read, buying behavior and what they value. One is the "fanatic" who brings most revenue and brings friends; others are secondary. Example: "The Beer Fanatic" (about 70% of taproom revenue), "The Beer Snob" (Curt, 35, tech engineer, trades bottles, rejects brands bought by big brewers), "The Brewery Enthusiast" (Jessica, 32, two kids, side business, shops at Trader Joe's).

## 4. Before and after grid (8-Stage Offer Plan)
People do not buy products. They buy a transformation and status (nobody buys a Rolex to tell the time).

| | Before (without you) | After (with you) |
|---|---|---|
| Have | what they own or use now | what they will have |
| Feel | how that makes them feel | how they will feel |
| Average day | how their day goes because of it | their new average day |
| Status | how others see them, what is said behind their back | how they are seen now |
| Good vs evil | the "evil" your product fights in their world | the good that wins |

Most brands stop at "Have". The strongest hooks come from Average day, Status and Good vs evil. Turn each cell into a hook ("Get your kids off Snapchat and hit the lake").

## 5. Jobs to be done (external: Christensen, Moesta)
- Job: "When <situation>, I want to <motivation>, so I can <outcome>."
- Three layers: functional (get it done), emotional (how I feel), social (how others see me).
- Forces of progress: push of the current situation, pull of the new solution, against anxiety about the new and habit of the present. Copy should raise push and pull and lower anxiety and habit.

## 6. Customer questions in order (8-Stage Offer Plan, TBIF)
Once the hook wins attention, the client asks:
1. Function: How does this improve my life?
2. Brand: Do I like and trust the people selling it? (Mission, values, founder story.)
3. Influence: Do others vouch for it? (Reviews, clients, endorsements, years, partners.)
4. Transaction: Does the perceived value outweigh the cost? (Bundles, bonuses, guarantee, payment terms.)
Every page, ad and email should answer these four.

## 7. Three questions before any sale
1. Does the client need what you sell? Find the problem first.
2. Do they like you and need you? People buy from people they like.
3. Only then: product and price that solve the problem.
