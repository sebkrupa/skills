# Marketing, channels and content

## 1. Fundamentals
- Diagnose first: small list, low traffic, or low conversion? More traffic does not fix a weak offer. High views with low sales means a broken funnel, checkout or offer (Brendan Kane).
- One channel first. Make it profitable (know cost per lead, cost per client, lifetime value; aim for at least 50% return), then add channels (Suby).
- Owner's time (King's Audit, Suby): hourly rate = income ÷ hours. Spend your time on the 4% that makes money (offers, copy, funnels, content, sales); delegate the rest.
- Channels and tactics change; fundamentals do not.

## 2. Traffic temperature (Suby)
"The temperature of the message must match the temperature of the traffic."
| Traffic | Who | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Cold ("Tinder") | Strangers | Value first: lead magnet, education. No hard pitch. |
| Warm ("second date") | Know you, not sold | Nurture: proof, quick wins, invitation to talk |
| Hot ("Netflix and chill") | Clients and fans | Personal: offers, upgrades, referral asks |
Only about 3% of a market is buying now. Educate the rest so you are the one they call when ready.
Funnel jobs: the ad sells the click; the opt-in page sells the opt-in; the landing page sells the next step; the call, webinar or checkout makes the sale.

## 3. Lead magnet (HVCO, Suby)
- Solves the client's single most burning problem with the most useful quick solution. Real value, no promotional brochure. Goal: "If this is free, imagine the paid work."
- Short is fine (5-8 page PDF, checklist, video, quiz, calculator, template, mini course). Ask only name and email.
- Headline matters most. Add a "Why are we publishing this?" box to answer scepticism.
- Maths: 100 visitors to "get a quote" → about 1 sale; 100 to a good lead magnet with nurture → about 3.

## 4. Nurture (Magic Lantern, Suby)
Draw the path from the client's current state to the desired outcome. Mark 4-5 milestones. Make one piece of content per milestone that gives a real result. 80% value, 20% pitch; end with an invitation to book a call. Phone-quality video is fine; value beats production.

## 5. Social content (Brendan Kane)
- Platforms reward retention, because they sell ads. Views count after about 3 seconds on Instagram and TikTok.
- Generalist principle: explain niche expertise so a broad audience finds it interesting. Reach grows faster than the share of ideal clients drops (10k views × 50% fit = 5k; 1M × 10% = 100k). Example: a realtor's videos about $2.7M closets sold a $30M house.
- Formats over trends. A format is a repeatable story structure; trends die in weeks, formats live for years. Master one format.
- Gold / Silver / Bronze analysis: pick a creator in the format; compare 10 top videos (millions of views), 10-20 mid, and weak ones. Compare context, editing and the first 3-5 seconds, not the topic.
- Reverse production: decide the hook and conclusion first, then script or interview toward it. Clear visual hierarchy of text and image in the first seconds.
- Trust builds organically; conversion follows. Education beat ads for a craftsman: site visits from 10k to 100k.
- Under NDA: ask permission to mention the client; otherwise make anonymous lessons from the project.

## 6. LinkedIn (Jasmin Alic)
- Profile is a landing page with one CTA (e.g. booking link in Featured). The headline shows in feed, comments and messages and is cut at about 45 characters: put the clear offer there, not a slogan. Banner and headline say the same thing.
- Post: hook line, blank line, rehook line (only about 3 lines show before "see more"). Aim for at least 30 seconds of dwell time. A face photo at 4:5 beats impersonal graphics. Rhythm of lines (1-2-1-2). Link the photo detail to the text metaphor. End by inviting replies.
- Comment 20-50 times a day on bigger creators' posts. Write for all readers, like a post-credits scene. Rule 1 + 3: one strong main comment plus three replies to others. Early comments on viral posts borrow their reach.
- Warm outreach: at least 10 useful comments before a connection request or DM.

## 7. Referrals and partners (Joanna Galvao, "Book Solid")
- About 91% of B2B buyers are influenced by word of mouth. A referral opens the door. Paid funnels can fail in services (her agency: $30k funnel + $3k ads, zero clients).
- Three lists: (1) old leads who never bought: send short "is this still a priority?" emails, about 50 a day (900 contacts gave 6 calls and 6 clients a week); (2) best clients: ask each for one specific referral; (3) partners serving the same client (copywriters, photographers, SEO, ads specialists).
- Big fish strategy: build a relationship with a well-connected authority without pitching. Give strategic value first (free work only for key people while learning, never for those who exploit it).
- Stay in touch after projects: introduce useful people, send articles, comment on their posts, share ideas, meet for coffee.
- Publish case studies, testimonials and measurable results.
- Systems: package the offer, publish scope and price, a visible pipeline (lead in → contact → call booked → proposal → won/lost), email templates, a 15-minute call with 3x Why. Delight with surprises (welcome postcard, birthday gifts). Document processes; delegate with IPO (information, permission, outcome).

## 8. Retention and word of mouth (product brands)
Share-worthy products and packaging, a thank-you card, a small surprise, a review request, referral cards ("tag us for 10% off"), a private community or VIP club, thank-you email with a next-order discount, seasonal and birthday emails. Instagram, Facebook (marketplace, shop) for small product brands.

## 9. Mental availability (external: Byron Sharp)
For brands with many buyers: reach all category buyers, not only loyal ones; be present in buying situations; keep distinctive assets consistent for years; be easy to buy.

## 10. Website structure (default)
- Home: who it is for + result (ROT headline), subheadline with proof, 3 problems, the plan in 3 steps, offer summary, proof, guarantee, CTA. Pass the 5-second test: what, for whom, why you, what to do next.
- Offer or service pages (one per core offer): before and after, deliverables, process, price or starting price, FAQ with objections, CTA.
- Case studies: client, problem, approach, measurable result, quote.
- About: origin story, mission and enemy, the person behind the brand.
- Lead magnet landing page; contact or booking page with qualifying questions.
- Content hub (articles, videos) organized by content pillars, each answering real client questions (helps search and AI answers).
